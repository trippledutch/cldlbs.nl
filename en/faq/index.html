Access and setup

Does my cluster have to be measured read-only?

No. CloudLabs works remote-first via a secure session (Microsoft Teams, AnyDesk, or equivalent). No agents or monitoring software are installed on your servers. All PowerShell scripts are run by you, or visibly under supervision, you remain in control at all times.

What data does a health check collect?

Read-only access to the Hyper-V hosts and cluster is sufficient for the core inventory and analysis. Broader scope adds read-only accounts where relevant: out-of-band (BMC) for the server hardware, the storage array, the switch fabric, and Azure ARM (Reader) for Azure Local. For remediation steps you will share a session so that commands are always executed by someone on your side. Credentials are never stored.

Scope and duration

How long does a cluster assessment take?

The measurement itself takes about fifteen minutes: a collection script runs from your own management server over WinRM to the cluster nodes and only reads. The result files then go into the CloudLabs Studio and come back as a finished report, normally within three to five working days. The intake sets the scope and happens once, at the start.

Does this work for Azure Local (formerly Azure Stack HCI)?

Yes. CloudLabs covers S2D (Storage Spaces Direct) and Azure Local alongside traditional Hyper-V clusters. A readiness assessment for migration to Azure Local is also a standard service, including firmware baseline, driver compatibility, and network prerequisites.

How do I prepare for a cluster assessment?

Very little. A brief topology overview (number of hosts, storage type, Windows version) is helpful for scoping. The intake call fills in the rest. No formal documentation is required upfront.

How often should a cluster be checked?

A health check is not a one-off. After remediation each follow-up round re-runs the same read-only measurement and reports the delta plus a risk trend chart, so progress is measured, not asserted. Two or three rounds a year is a common rhythm.

How do I know if my Azure Local cluster is healthy?

You do not know that from the management console. Firmware drift, lopsided CSV ownership and deviating PFC classes are not visible in Failover Cluster Manager and give no warning. They emerge from a measurement.

De gratis Cluster Triage only reads your environment and gives the five heaviest signals around an incident window. The Base measurement gives the complete picture, with a findings report and a one-hour review.

Why does a two-node cluster run without redundancy during patching?

Because during a patch round there is always a node out of rotation. With two nodes that means: one node carries everything and there is no second one to fall back on if something goes wrong in that window.

That is exactly why the measurement records the firmware level and the vote distribution per node: a patch round that is safe on paper may just not be safe in practice. Zie ook het artikel over Cluster-Aware Updating.

Deliverable

Do I get a report I can hand to my customer?

A customer-facing .docx report with findings ranked by severity and recommended remediation steps. On a follow-up measurement you also receive a Delta report and a remaining-risk overview with a risk trend chart. As-Built system documentation and, after an incident, a Root Cause Analysis are available as separate deliverables. On request: an action list (plain text, ready to paste into your ticketing system) and the raw PowerShell inventory output. Reports are delivered in Dutch, English, German, Spanish or French, whichever you prefer.

Is an NDA standard?

Yes. An NDA is signed before any work starts. All inventory output and configuration data stays with you, it is never stored by CloudLabs beyond the duration of the engagement.

In which languages does CloudLabs deliver reports?

All reporting is delivered in Dutch, English, German, Spanish or French. The language is fixed at the intake and applies to the whole engagement.

Delivery is in the standard CloudLabs layout, in an editable format. If you want to hand over the report in your own house style or the customer's, you apply that layout yourself.

What is an as-built document?

Current system documentation, generated from the latest measurement: identity, hardware, storage, network, cluster, virtual machines, management and backup, down to firmware levels and VLAN tables.

Always in agreement with reality, because it is derived from reality. Because it is generated again at every measurement, documentation drift disappears: a replaced switch, an extra VLAN or a new node simply appears in the next version.

/ After an incident

Can anyone analyse my cluster logs after an incident?

The collector runs from your management server, read-only. Per node it reads the event logs across the incident window: 29 channels, insofar as they are switched on in your cluster. The timelines of the nodes are laid over each other and corrected for clock difference.

Zie investigation after an incident for exactly what is collected and what you receive.

How do I prove that an incident was not caused by my configuration?

With a reconstruction that puts the configuration next to the events. That is why we advise the investigation in combination with a base measurement: a deviating firmware level or a skewed network setting cannot otherwise be told apart from coincidence.

You receive an RCA document with the incident window, the timeline across all nodes, the established cause insofar as the evidence carries it, and the gaps with name and reason.

Techniek

What is the difference between Azure Stack HCI and Azure Local?

Azure Local is Microsoft's current name for the operating system previously called Azure Stack HCI. A hyperconverged Windows platform managed through Azure Arc, billed per core per month and intended for production workloads that have to stay on premises.

See the article on Azure Local migratie readiness for what makes 2026 different in that respect.
