The HealthCheck

From measurement to report
four times a year

A one-year cycle: a base measurement and three follow-up measurements, one per quarter. Every measurement produces a report and an hour in which we walk you through it.

If you want your cluster checked, this is the service: a cluster health check for Hyper-V, Failover Cluster, Storage Spaces Direct and Azure Local. A Hyper-V cluster assessment, a failover cluster assessment, a Storage Spaces Direct assessment and an Azure Local health check all four run on the same measurement; what differs is which modules run along.

An independent assessment of your cluster, usable as a second opinion on a cluster design, as a cluster audit, or as a risk inventory before you expand. Anyone who wants to know whether their cluster is production ready starts here.

The free Cluster Triage is the first step and costs you nothing. Walk through the cycle: the figure follows along.

  1. Step 0 · one-off

    Intake

    For the first measurement Get-ClusterIntake.ps1 reads the identity of the cluster: cluster name, nodes, management subnet and domain. The engagement thus starts from measured data instead of retyped data, and a field that could not be read reports itself with the reason. This happens once, at the start.

  2. The measurement

    Read-only, from your own management server

    A collection script runs from your own management server, over WinRM, to the cluster nodes. The script only reads and changes nothing. The base measurement takes about fifteen minutes.

    • Nothing is installed: no agent, no service, no scheduled task. Nothing is left behind afterwards.
    • The script changes no settings and restarts nothing.
    • No passwords are processed or stored. The script works with the privileges of the administrator who starts it, and before the transfer file is written it checks its own output for values that look like credentials.
    • The result is a file on your server. It stays with you until you hand it over to us, through an encrypted Proton share we set up for you.

    The modules run separately, with access agreed with you in advance, and read what the base measurement cannot see: the storage array, the switches, the management controllers and the Azure side.

  3. Step 1

    Import.

    The measurement is imported and laid beside the previous one, so it is visible what is new, what is open and what has been resolved.

  4. Step 2

    Author.

    Every finding is built from the measured values of this run, not from a template.

  5. Step 3

    Assess.

    An assessor reads the whole, weighs the risk levels and adjusts them where needed. Every adjustment is recorded with its reason.

  6. Step 4

    Check.

    An automated check runs across the whole report. The check in step 4 holds the document back as long as something is missing. It refuses a report in which:

    • a finding contains no measured values: no numbers, no node names, no date;
    • a high risk lacks its action item;
    • a passage appears that would fit any report;
    • a part of the measurement was neither assessed nor declared out of scope.

    As long as one of these points is open, no document comes out. With many suppliers the quality of a report depends on who wrote it that week; here a check stands in between.

  7. On refusal

    Back to writing

    If the report is held back, it does not go on to approval but back to step 2. This is the only moment at which the process runs backwards.

  8. Step 5

    Approve.

    The assessor signs. If the text changes after that, the signature lapses and it is signed again.

  9. Step 6

    Render.

    The document is rendered in the languages you choose.

  10. The result

    What the report contains

    Per finding you read what was measured, with the values, the node names and the date; what the vendor or Microsoft prescribes, with the source; where those two diverge and what that means in your situation; and the repair.

    With the base measurement you receive the full findings report. With every follow-up measurement you receive a delta report: what is new, what is still open and what has been resolved.

  11. The review

    One hour in which we go through it with you

    Every measurement produces a report and an hour in which we walk you through it.

  12. The cycle

    Every quarter again

    A cycle of one year: a base measurement and three follow-up measurements, one each quarter. One year is C1 + V1 + V2 + V3: four measurements, four reports, four reviews.

    A single measurement shows how your environment stands at that moment. By repeating the same measurement every quarter, the development becomes visible too. In each next report you see what has been resolved, with the date on which the repair was measured: the same check that found the deviation no longer finds it.

    You also see what stays open: a finding that has stood for three quarters weighs heavier than a finding from last week. And you see small shifts before they become an incident.
