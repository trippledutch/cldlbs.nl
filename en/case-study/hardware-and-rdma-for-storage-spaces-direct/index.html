Case study 03 · base measurement with modules M1 and M3

Three NIC models, taken off the order list

Logistics· 40+ virtual machines· SAN → Storage Spaces Direct· Hyper-V· Baselinequarter

Over forty virtual machines ran on ageing SAN-based Hyper-V, while a hardware replacement was on the planning. The company wanted to know whether Storage Spaces Direct was a sensible step and had no in-house knowledge of S2D. The measurement was not about the existing cluster but about the order list: three of the intended network card models were not on the list of validated configurations and were replaced before the purchase order went out. On top of that the PFC classes between host and switch turned out not to match, the kind of deviation that shows up later as slow storage and not as a network fault.

01

The situation

A logistics company with over forty virtual machines on Hyper-V with SAN storage. The hardware was reaching the end of its support term and budget for replacement was available. The vendor had proposed Storage Spaces Direct; internally there was nobody who could judge whether that was right.

That is the most dangerous phase of such a track. The decision that is most expensive to reverse is taken at the moment when nothing has been measured yet. Until the last moment an order list looks like an administrative step, while it fixes the reliability of the coming five years.

02

The measurement

The base measurement mapped the existing environment. The modules for Ethernet switches and the storage system also put the intended configuration next to the requirements S2D sets.

Why this counts

Storage Spaces Direct builds the storage layer in software out of the disks and network cards of the hosts themselves. With a SAN the reliability sits with the storage system; with S2D it sits with the combination of components in your own servers. A card that supports RDMA on paper but is not validated can drop packets under load that the cluster sees as a storage fault. That cannot be repaired with a setting: it is the wrong card.

Action point

Take the three models off the order list and replace them with validated models, before the purchase order. See also the comparison of SAN, S2D and Azure Local.

Why this counts

RoCE expects a network that drops no packets. Priority Flow Control arranges that by pausing traffic in a class instead of discarding it. If the host is on a different class than the switch, that pause does not arrive where it is needed and traffic is dropped after all. The cluster then reports no network fault but slow or failing storage. That sends the investigation in the wrong direction.

Action point

Put host and switch on the same priority class and record the reservation. The network design for RDMA was delivered and gone through with the network team; they applied and validated the configuration themselves.

03

What could not be measured

Every CloudLabs report states what stayed out of reach, with the reason. Not because it looks good, but because otherwise a reader does not know how complete the picture is.

  • The new hardware itself: not yet delivered at the time of measurement
  • Behaviour of S2D under production load: only determinable after the migration
  • Firmware of the existing storage system: reason

04

The repair

  • The order list was adjusted: three models replaced by validated alternatives, before the purchase.
  • A network design for RDMA was delivered; the network team applied it and validated the configuration.
  • A migration runbook was written; all forty-plus virtual machines were migrated without data loss.

What the customer left alone: finding not taken up, with the customer’s reason. That is stated, because an example in which everything is resolved is not an example but an advertisement.

05

The outcome

The migration ran as planned. But the value sat almost entirely in the moment at which the measurement was done. After the purchase a wrong NIC model would have been a write-off, or an awkward exchange with a vendor who could maintain that the model supports RDMA. Before it, it was a line on a list.

The deviating PFC classes would not have reported themselves as a network fault but as storage that collapses under load: the kind of problem teams lose weeks to because they are looking in the wrong place.

06

Questions

Why is the hardware list so decisive with Storage Spaces Direct?

Because S2D builds the storage layer in software out of the disks and network cards of the hosts themselves. With a SAN the reliability sits with the storage system; with S2D it sits with the combination of components in your own servers.

A network card that supports RDMA on paper but is not validated can drop packets under load that the cluster sees as a storage fault. That cannot be solved with a setting.

What goes wrong if PFC classes between host and switch do not match?

RoCE expects a network that drops no packets. Priority Flow Control arranges that by pausing traffic in a class instead of discarding it. If the host is on a different class than the switch, that pause does not arrive where it is needed.

The cluster then sees no network fault but slow storage. That sends the investigation in the wrong direction.

Can CloudLabs do this before we order hardware?

Yes. That is also the moment at which it delivers the most. After the purchase a wrong model is a write-off; before it, it is a line on an order list.
