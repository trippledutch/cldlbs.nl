Case study 02 · base measurement

A quorum that would not survive the second site

Healthcare· Two datacenter sites· Failover Cluster· Windows Server 2019 → 2022· Baselinequarter

A hospital group with two datacenter sites planned a rolling upgrade from Windows Server 2019 to 2022, keeping clinical systems available. An independent judgement was asked on the order of the upgrade. The measurement looked somewhere else first and found the heaviest finding there: the vote distribution across the two sites would not have survived the loss of site B. And during a rolling upgrade, with a node out of rotation each time, that margin was smaller still. The quorum was corrected before the first node was touched.

01

The situation

Two datacenter sites, clinical systems that must not go down and an upgrade path that had to be walked once. The internal team had worked out the rolling upgrade themselves and wanted to know whether the order was right: which node first, how long between the steps and where the way back lay if a node did not come back.

That is a sensible question. It just was not the question that delivered the most. A rolling upgrade temporarily changes the composition of the cluster: there is a node out of rotation each time. Whether that is safe depends not on the order but on what remains if something else goes wrong during that period as well.

02

The measurement

The collection script ran from the management server over WinRM to all nodes at both sites: read-only, about fifteen minutes. The heaviest finding was not about the upgrade.

Why this counts

In a cluster across two sites the question is not how many nodes fail, but which. If the witness sits at one of the two sites, that witness effectively decides which site may keep running. If you lose the site where it stands, the whole cluster stops, including the nodes that are doing fine at the other site. A witness belongs in a third place, separate from both datacenters.

Action point

Move the witness to a place separate from both sites and re-establish the vote distribution, before the upgrade starts. See also the comparison of witness types.

Why this counts

A rolling upgrade is not a brief moment but a period of days. During that period the margin in the quorum is smaller than normal. A second event then does not have to be large to bring the cluster down: a node that does not come back, or a network interruption between the sites.

Action point

Rearrange the order so that at no moment two votes are missing at the same site at once, with the way back recorded for each step.

03

What could not be measured

Every CloudLabs report states what stayed out of reach, with the reason. Not because it looks good, but because otherwise a reader does not know how complete the picture is.

  • The link between the two sites: managed by a third party; out of scope
  • Switch configuration: outside the scope of the base measurement; covered by module M1
  • Behaviour of the storage on loss of a site: reason

04

The repair

  • The witness was moved to a place separate from both sites; the vote distribution was re-established and tested.
  • The upgrade order was rearranged, with a recorded way back for each step.
  • A node-by-node playbook was delivered, which the internal team carried out themselves.
  • Afterwards a validation report was delivered stating that the cluster runs on functional level 2022 and the quorum stands as intended.

What the customer left alone: finding not taken up, with the customer’s reason. That is stated, because an example in which everything is resolved is not an example but an advertisement.

05

The outcome

The upgrade was completed without unplanned interruption. That is the outcome the team wanted, but not the most important one: the critical finding stood apart from the upgrade and had been there for years. Without a measurement it would only have been found at the moment site B actually dropped out, not as a finding but as an incident.

For the IT manager the value sat in the validation report: a document that shows the board that the upgrade was done, what was checked and what was found in order.

06

Questions

Why is quorum across two sites harder than at one site?

Because the question is not how many nodes fail, but which. A cluster with an even number of nodes spread across two sites has the same number of votes on both sides. If the link between the sites drops, neither half keeps a majority, unless there is a third vote elsewhere.

If the witness sits at one of the two sites, it effectively decides which site may keep running. If you lose that site, the whole cluster stops.

Why does a rolling upgrade not check the quorum itself?

A cluster rolling upgrade looks at the functional level of the cluster and at the nodes taking part, not at the question of whether the vote distribution survives the loss of a site.

During the upgrade there is moreover a node out of rotation each time. A configuration that just about holds in daily operation can, during the upgrade, be missing exactly the vote it needs.

Does the measurement affect production?

No. The script runs for about fifteen minutes from the management server over WinRM, only reads, installs nothing and leaves nothing behind. No maintenance window is needed.
