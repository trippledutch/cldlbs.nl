CloudLabs Health Check Toolchain v2

Every CloudLabs health check runs on the same machinery: a tailored, read-only inventory script at the customer, processed in the CloudLabs Studio into documents a customer can act on. That machinery is now in its second generation, and partners can run engagements on it.

What it is

The CloudLabs Health Check Toolchain v2 is a measurement chain, not a monitoring product. Nothing gets installed. A PowerShell script, built for the specific environment during a short intake, runs once with read-only credentials the customer provides. The results go to the CloudLabs Studio, where every data point is checked against a maintained catalogue of well over a hundred distinct checks: Microsoft baselines, vendor firmware floors, fabric configuration rules, known failure patterns.

One thing the tooling never does is draw conclusions. It measures, compares and ranks; a specialist reads the evidence and writes the findings. Every finding states what was measured, what good looks like, how far the environment deviates, why the risk level is what it is, and a concrete action plan with verification steps. Where something was checked and found in order, the report says that too, so the customer can see exactly what was covered.

What v2 measures

The first generation covered Windows Server, failover clustering and Hyper-V. Version 2 extends the reach to most of the modern datacenter stack.

01
Platform

Windows Server & clusters

Windows Server up to 2025, failover clustering, Hyper-V, Cluster-Aware Updating, live migration configuration, time service, patch currency, OS lifecycle including ESU exposure.

02
Azure Local · HCI

Azure Local & S2D

Azure Local, HCI and Storage Spaces Direct: pool and virtual disk health, Network ATC intents, Arc connectivity, secured-core posture, failover headroom. Azure Migrate where relevant.

03
Hardware

Server hardware

Dell, HPE and Lenovo, measured out-of-band through the BMC: firmware against verified security floors, power supply redundancy, hardware event logs, uniformity across nodes.

04
Storage

Storage arrays

Pure Storage, HPE, Dell and Lenovo arrays, read-only. Host-to-array symmetry: does what the array serves match what the hosts actually see.

05
Network

Switch fabric & HBAs

Ethernet switches across several network operating systems, plus Ethernet HBAs. Host-versus-switch parity checks: PFC classes, VLAN trunks, the places where lossless RDMA quietly stops being lossless.

06
Management

Management & backup

VMM, Windows Admin Center including WAC Virtualization Mode, Veeam and Commvault can all be taken into the measurement.

By request. Missing a module for hardware or software your customer runs? Additional modules can usually be added at short notice.

The documents

An engagement produces four document types, with a fifth available on call. Every page below is real output from the sample engagement, click to view full size.

DOC 01

Baseline health check

The first measurement. Full findings with risk analysis: executive summary, a findings table graded HIGH, MEDIUM and LOW, a register of what was checked and found in order, and prioritised recommendations. The summary table gives management the whole picture on one page; the chapters behind it give the engineers the detail.

DOC 02

Delta report

After each follow-up measurement: what was resolved, what stayed open, what is new. Resolved findings are verified by measurement, not by ticket status; a finding only turns green when the follow-up run proves the fix. This is the document that keeps you at the table between measurements.

DOC 03

Remaining risks with progress chart

The open risks that still need attention, with a trend chart showing the progression across measurements. Tangible proof of work done, in a form a customer can put in front of management, an auditor or a cyber insurer.

DOC 04

As-Built documentation

Current system documentation, generated from the latest measurement: identity, hardware, storage, network, cluster, virtual machines, management and backup, down to firmware levels and VLAN tables. Always in sync with reality, because it is derived from reality.

DOC 05

Root Cause Analysis, on call

After a crash or serious incident: a standard measurement plus a read-only incident harvest, folded into a cross-node timeline. The analysis names the most likely cause, shows the alternatives that were weighed and rejected, and states its own evidence gaps. RCAs are handled with priority: same day or the next working day.

Reports are available in Dutch and English, and can be delivered in your house style or the customer's. The rhythm that works: a baseline, then two or three follow-up measurements a year, working down the finding list in between.

How an engagement runs

01

Intake

A short session to pin down scope and the specifics of the environment. The result is an inventory script built for exactly that configuration.

02

Measurement

You run the script at your customer, independently. It executes read-only commands with an account the customer provides. Switches, storage arrays and Azure ARM may need additional read-only accounts.

03

Processing and reporting

The result files go into the CloudLabs Studio and come back as finished documents, normally within three to five working days. Processing happens on a hosted server in the Netherlands; the data stays within Dutch jurisdiction.

04

Review and follow-up

The report is discussed with the customer. The findings are then worked down by the customer and you, with CloudLabs on call for the specialist parts. That follow-up work is yours; the toolchain keeps producing the evidence of progress.

Your customer stays your customer. CloudLabs never approaches your customer without you, unless you explicitly ask for it. CloudLabs expertise is available to you and to the customer throughout the engagement.

A worked example: Noorderlicht

Noorderlicht B.V. is not a real customer. It is a fictional environment, built specifically so the toolchain's output can be shown without exposing anyone's production data. The documents themselves are real: the same pipeline, the same house style, the same rules as a paying engagement. Below is one finding from the baseline report, in full. This is the level of detail every finding carries: the measured situation with versions and dates, the best practice with its source, the deviation and why it earns this risk level, and an action plan an engineer can execute, including the verification that closes the loop.

Azure Local · 4 nodes

AZLCL01: baseline and two follow-ups

A four-node Azure Local cluster: all-flash S2D, 25 GbE RoCE storage network, out-of-band hardware measurement, switch fabric and interview evidence included. Baseline in May 2026: 32 findings, of which 3 HIGH, 11 MEDIUM, 6 LOW, and 12 items checked and found in order.

HIGH: BMC firmware below the vendor security floor on all four nodes.
HIGH: PFC priority mismatch between hosts and one switch, so the lossless RDMA guarantee did not hold end to end.
HIGH: two guest servers on Windows Server 2012 R2 without extended support cover, with a hard deadline months away.
Two follow-up measurements later: zero HIGH findings open, twelve findings measured as resolved.
RCA · 6 nodes

PRODCL01: root cause analysis of a Saturday outage

A six-node S2D cluster lost a CSV on a Saturday afternoon; 14 VMs restarted. The RCA reconstructed the chain from cross-node event evidence: a switch had been replaced two days earlier and its lossless storage configuration was never restored. Under combined backup and repair traffic, storage I/O timed out and a node was evicted.

Every causal claim in the timeline cites its evidence.
Three alternative explanations weighed and rejected, with the reasons stated.
Gaps named explicitly: the replaced switch only held 48 hours of logs.
The report links back to the health check register: the change-without-verification risk was already on it.
